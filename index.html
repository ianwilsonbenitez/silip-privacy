Privacy Policy for Silip

Last updated: September 21, 2026

Silip is an app that helps you keep track of when your government IDs, professional licenses, and business permits expire. It is made by an individual developer, not a company.

Silip is an independent, personal document-tracking tool. It is not an official government app and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or authorized by any Philippine government agency (including the DFA, LTO, PRC, NBI, DTI, BIR, or any local government unit). Document type names and agency references in the app are used only to help you identify what you're tracking.

This policy is short because the app does very little with your data: everything you put into Silip stays on your phone.

The short version

  • Silip has no server, no account, and no login.
  • Nothing you enter is uploaded or transmitted anywhere by the app.
  • The developer cannot see, access, or receive your data. There is nowhere for it to go.
  • There are no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting, and no third-party tracking in the app.
  • Your data is never sold or shared, because it is never collected in the first place.

What Silip stores, and where

All of the following lives only in Silip's own private storage on your device (a local database that belongs to the app).

Document records. For each document you track, Silip stores:

  • the document type (one of the built-in types, such as Passport, Driver's License, or PWD ID, or a "Custom" type you name yourself)
  • a label you give it
  • the issue date and expiry date
  • an "agency" field (free text)
  • a "notes" field (free text)
  • an owner tag: a free-text label like "Me", "Spouse", or a name you choose. This is just a way for you to organise your own documents. It is not a user account, and Silip has no concept of separate users.
  • whether reminders are switched on for that document
  • an optional photo (see below)

About the "notes" field. Many people use this field to write down ID numbers, reference numbers, or other details. Silip never reads, validates, parses, or interprets what you type there. It is stored exactly as you wrote it and shown back to you. Because that field can hold your most sensitive information, what goes in it is your choice and your responsibility to protect.

Photos. You may attach one photo to a document, for example a picture of the ID or permit itself. You can take it with the camera or pick it from your photo library. The photo file is copied into Silip's own private app storage on your device. It is never uploaded anywhere, and it is not included in backup exports (see below). When you remove a photo, or delete the document it belongs to, the file is deleted from your device.

Reminders. Silip schedules reminder notifications on your device: 90, 30, and 7 days before a document expires, plus a 6-month heads-up for passports. These are local notifications scheduled by your phone's own operating system. There is no push server and no device token sent to anyone. Silip does not know whether you ever saw or tapped a reminder.

Your birthday (optional, set in Settings). If you choose to enter your birthday, Silip stores only the month and day, never the year, since the year isn't needed for anything the app does with it. This is used to suggest a more accurate expiry date for a PRC License or Driver's License that belongs to "Me" specifically (both are, by law, renewed on the licensee's birthday). It is never applied to a Spouse's or Child's documents, since it would be the wrong birthday for them. Like everything else, it is stored only on your device and is never transmitted anywhere.

App Lock (optional, set in Settings). If you turn this on, Silip asks for a PIN and/or Face ID / Touch ID / fingerprint before it opens. Your PIN is never stored as plain text, only a one-way cryptographic hash of it is kept on your device, and that hash can't be turned back into your PIN. For biometrics, Silip never sees, receives, or stores your actual fingerprint or face data at all: your phone's own operating system handles the scan entirely on its own and only tells Silip "yes" or "no." There's no account behind this feature, so if you forget your PIN, the only way back in is to clear all data on this device (see below).

A few minor technical preferences, such as your light/dark theme choice and the timestamp of your last backup export, are also kept on your device, purely so the app can remember your settings between launches. These are not personal information and are never transmitted anywhere either.

Backup files you create yourself

Silip has an Export backup feature. When you tap it, the app builds a JSON file containing your document records (the fields listed above, not your photos) and hands it to your phone's standard share sheet. From there, you decide where it goes: AirDrop, Google Drive, email, the Files app, a messaging app, or anywhere else. Silip does not upload the file, does not choose the destination, and does not keep a copy anywhere outside your device.

Once that file leaves your phone, it is an ordinary, unencrypted file under your control, and whatever privacy rules apply to the place you sent it apply to your data. Please think before you send a backup to a shared drive, a group chat, or an email account other people can read. The file may contain ID numbers you typed into the notes field. The same caution applies to screenshots of the app.

Import works the other way around: you pick a JSON file using your phone's own file picker, Silip asks you to confirm, and then it replaces the data currently in the app with the contents of that file. The app only ever reads the one file you choose.

Permissions Silip asks for

Silip asks for as little as possible, and only at the moment it is actually needed.

  • Camera: only when you tap "Add photo" and choose to take a picture. Used solely to capture that one photo.
  • Photo library: only when you tap "Add photo" and choose an existing image. Used solely to let you pick that one photo. Silip does not scan, browse, or index your photo library.
  • Notifications: so the app can show you expiry reminders on your device. If you say no, the rest of the app still works; you just will not get reminders.

You can withdraw any of these permissions at any time in your phone's system settings.

Silip does not request or use location, contacts, microphone, calendar, health data, or your device's advertising identifier.

Third parties

There are none. Silip contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting service, and no social login. No third party receives your data through this app.

The only outside parties ever involved are the ones you choose yourself, for example, the cloud service or app you send an exported backup to. Those services have their own privacy policies, and Silip has no control over them.

Children's privacy

Silip is not directed at children, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included. The app has no accounts, no sign-up, and no way to send information to the developer, so there is no data about any user, of any age, for the developer to receive or store.

How to delete your data

Because your data never leaves your device, deleting it is entirely in your hands:

  • Delete individual documents inside the app. Deleting a document also deletes any photo attached to it.
  • Use the "Clear all data" control in Settings, which erases every document Silip has stored on your device.
  • Uninstall Silip. Removing the app removes its private storage, including all document records and attached photos.

Any backup files you exported earlier are separate copies that live wherever you sent them. You have to delete those yourself. The app cannot reach them.

Since the developer holds no copy of your data, there is nothing to request, correct, or delete on the developer's side. There is no account to close.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes how it handles data (for example, if a future version adds an optional online feature), this policy will be updated and the "last updated" date above will change. Any such change would be described plainly before it took effect.
